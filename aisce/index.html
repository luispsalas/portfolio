AI Strategic Context Engine

A framework I designed for turning scattered project information into strategic context that people and AI models can both use.

  • Own framework
  • v3.5
  • Used in client sessions
  • ES / EN

The problem

Most attempts to use AI strategically stall in the same place. The bottleneck is rarely the model's intelligence; it is the quality of the context the model receives. Project knowledge sits in people's heads, meeting notes and half-finished documents, and an AI model given a vague brief returns generic advice.

The engine is a guided way to build that context first: a structured map of what is being attempted, for whom, why and under what constraints, before any tool or AI system is chosen.

How it works

A facilitated session moves through eight modules. Any module can be skipped, and the session runs in Spanish or English.

  1. Project context: what kind of project it is, its scale, horizon and setting, including the institutional context and whether it is for profit.
  2. Problem definition: from symptoms to a reformulated problem statement, with a stated level of confidence.
  3. Ecosystem and stakeholders: actors, audiences, competitors and narrative.
  4. Strategy and positioning: value proposition, strengths and risks, differentiation.
  5. AI and automation: which processes could be automated, and what role AI should play in each.
  6. Action plan: an operational roadmap that states, step by step, what a person does and what an AI system does.
  7. Devil's advocate: a deliberate challenge to the plan: risk scenarios, external factors, and the conditions under which the project should stop or be redesigned.
  8. Context package: a structured synthesis of everything above, written to be handed directly to an AI model.

Design principles

  • Everything feeds the context package. Each module exists because its answers make an AI model's later work better informed.
  • Human and AI roles are explicit. The plan never leaves it implicit who, or what, does a task.
  • Gaps over invented content. Outputs mark each statement as validated, hypothesis or pending, and an honest gap is preferred to a plausible guess.
  • Context before compliance. It works upstream of governance tooling: it establishes what is being governed, and why, before any compliance layer is applied.

What real sessions changed

The framework was first shaped for one person or a small team. Running it across three sessions with a team of six to eight people exposed gaps the design had not anticipated, and version 3.5 addressed them:

  • a team mode, with timing recalibrated for larger groups and a plan for several sessions from the start;
  • the diagnosis separated from the deliverables, so producing outputs does not compete with the thinking;
  • a way to capture off-topic input without derailing the module in progress;
  • and plain-language terms: English jargon is translated the first time it appears.

My role and where it stands

I designed the framework, run the sessions as facilitator, and revise it after each one. It currently runs as a guided AI workspace with no custom code; the next step is a standalone local app.
